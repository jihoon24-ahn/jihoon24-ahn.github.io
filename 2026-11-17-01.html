1. Describing what American teachers ask
Your wording"Every time I pronounce… many American teachers asked me to did you say R or L?"
Minimal correction"Every time I pronounce an R or L sound, many American teachers ask me, "Did you say R or L?""
Natural version"Whenever I say an R or an L, American teachers often ask, "Was that an R or an L?""
Why? This still happens, so use the present: ask, not asked. For a direct question, drop to and quote it, or report it: they ask me whether I said R or L.
2. Telling the Lululemon story
Your wording"The founder heard about Japanese people can't speak L sound, so I thought it was funny, so he made his brand name as many as he can."
Minimal correction"The founder heard that Japanese people can't say the L sound, and he thought it was funny, so he put as many L's in his brand name as he could."
Natural version"The founder heard that Japanese speakers struggle with L, thought it was funny, and put as many L's into the name as he could."
Why? Before a full clause, use heard that, not heard about. Keep the subject as he, not I. As many … as he could needs the noun inside it.
3. Waiting for news
Your wording"I'm waiting the greenhouse gas result."
Minimal correction"I'm waiting for the greenhouse gas result."
Natural version"I'm still waiting to hear back about the greenhouse gas audit."
Why? Wait needs for before the thing you expect. Hear back about is natural for results you're expecting from someone else.
4. Explaining executives' legal status
Your wording"They cannot be protected by Korean law, Korean labor law."
Minimal correction"They are not protected by Korean labor law."
Natural version"Unlike regular employees, they aren't protected by Korean labor law."
Why? Cannot be protected sounds like protection is impossible. You mean it doesn't apply to them: aren't protected by.
5. Explaining a diversity policy
Your wording"So, you know, in political correctiveness, so we have to show… Samsung has to show that we have a lot of women."
Minimal correction"Because of political correctness, Samsung has to show that it has a lot of women."
Natural version"For diversity reasons, the company has to show it has enough women in senior roles."
Why? The noun is political correctness. Start with because of to show the reason, and use it for the company, not we, when you speak as an outsider.
6. Explaining the timing of surgery
Your wording"They graduate the high school, and they are waiting for the results… That's the perfect time to do plastic surgery."
Minimal correction"They graduate from high school, and they are waiting for the results. That's the perfect time to do plastic surgery."
Natural version"After they graduate from high school and while they wait for their results, it's the perfect time to get plastic surgery."
Why? Graduate needs from, and high school takes no article. Patients get or have surgery; doctors do it.
7. Joking about a discount
Your wording"I talked to my wife that we can do plastic surgery with together, because it will be discounted."
Minimal correction"I told my wife that we could do plastic surgery together, because it would be discounted."
Natural version"I told my wife we should get plastic surgery together, since we'd get a discount."
Why? Talk to can't be followed by that; use told my wife that. In reported speech, can and will usually become could and would.

🗣️ Model Answer

How executives are evaluated at my company

At my company, executives are contract workers, so they don't have much job security. The evaluation period runs from January 1st to October 31st. In November, HR reviews each executive's results, and in December, they find out whether their contracts are renewed or they're let go. So November is evaluation season, and December is either a happy month or a sad one. Executives earn a much higher salary and bigger bonuses, but unlike regular employees, they aren't protected by Korean labor law. My executive has lasted about five years, which is quite unusual, so she must be really good.
