🚀 Priority Patterns & Suggestions

1. Control the timeline

Past stories sometimes shifted into the present or used an incomplete time structure, as in “it started from 9:30 to 10:30” and “when I first to go.”

Try: Use a three-part frame: background → past event → present result. Choose the tense before beginning each part.

  • “When I lived in Washington, DC, I attended the center regularly.”
  • “I met a tutor there, and we still talk on Zoom.”

2. Finish one clause before adding detail

Long answers contained several mid-sentence restarts. The ideas were strong, but the listener had to wait for the sentence structure to become clear.

Try: Speak in short thought groups. If a sentence breaks down, pause and use “Let me put that another way.”

  • “The volunteers are mostly retirees. Many worked for the government.”
  • “Let me put that another way. The program depends on volunteers.”

3. Use complete noun chunks

Articles and noun forms were sometimes missing or overbuilt, including “retiree people” and “have right to vote.”

Try: Learn the whole chunk, not the isolated noun: “a retiree,” “the right to,” and “funding.”

  • “Many of the volunteers are retirees.”
  • “Taxpayers should have the right to vote.”

4. Build around reliable collocations

Some verb–noun combinations were understandable but unnatural, such as “experiences from foreign culture” and “volunteers for running organizations.”

Try: Reuse fixed combinations such as “experience other cultures,” “run an organization,” and “recruit volunteers.”

  • “They wanted to share their experience of other cultures.”
  • “The center needs to recruit volunteers to run its programs.”

5. Frame nuanced opinions clearly

You discussed voting and immigration thoughtfully, but phrases such as “as an Asian view” made your position less precise than your reasoning.

Try: Use position + reason + qualification to sound balanced during sensitive discussions.

  • “From an East Asian perspective, showing ID at a polling place seems normal.”
  • “I see the concern, although I also think taxpayers deserve representation.”

✏️ Key Corrections

Tap a correction to see the revision and explanation.

1. Talking about bedtime
Your wording“I slept a little bit late.”
Minimal correction“I went to sleep a little late.”
Natural version“I stayed up a little later than usual.”
Why? Sleep late often means waking up late. Go to sleep late identifies bedtime; stay up late is the most natural conversational choice.
2. Describing a time range
Your wording“It started from 9:30 to 10:30 PM.”
Minimal correction“It started at 9:30 and ended at 10:30 PM.”
Natural version“It ran from 9:30 to 10:30 PM.”
Why? Use at with a starting time. The paired structure from … to … works naturally with run, last, or be.
3. An ongoing Zoom habit
Your wording“I usually, I regularly met one of my tutors on Zoom.”
Minimal correction“I regularly meet one of my tutors on Zoom.”
Natural version“I still meet one of my former tutors regularly on Zoom.”
Why? The meetings continue now, so use the present simple meet. Still clearly connects the current habit to the earlier school experience.
4. Referring to retired people
Your wording“There are many retiree people.”
Minimal correction“There are many retired people.”
Natural version“Many of the volunteers are retirees.”
Why? A retiree is already a person, so retiree people is redundant. Retirees is concise and directly identifies the group.
5. Experience with other cultures
Your wording“Many of them have had experiences from foreign culture.”
Minimal correction“Many of them have had experience with foreign cultures.”
Natural version“Many of them have lived or worked abroad and experienced other cultures.”
Why? General experience is usually uncountable, and the natural preposition is with. The natural version makes the type of experience concrete.
6. Using the passive voice
Your wording“It runs by another volunteers.”
Minimal correction“It is run by other volunteers.”
Natural version“Other volunteers run the organization.”
Why? The passive form needs be + past participle: is run. Use other, not another, before a plural noun.
7. Recalling a first visit
Your wording“When I first to go to the center …”
Minimal correction“When I first went to the center …”
Natural version“When I first started going to the center …”
Why? After when I first, use a finite past verb such as went. Started going is especially natural when describing the beginning of a repeated activity.
8. Expressing a voting right
Your wording“If you pay taxes, you might have right to vote.”
Minimal correction“If you pay taxes, you might have the right to vote.”
Natural version“If you pay taxes, you should have the right to vote.”
Why? The fixed expression is have the right to. Use should when stating what you believe is fair; might expresses uncertainty about a fact.

Why the Washington English Center Matters

When I lived in Washington, DC, I attended the Washington English Center. Many teachers were volunteers, including retirees who had worked for the federal government or international organizations. They wanted to share their experience and meet people from other cultures, so the program created a mutually beneficial exchange. Because many teachers were unpaid, the tuition was affordable. The center also helped immigrants understand practical issues such as visas and local voting. However, it has become harder to recruit volunteers, while students still need support. The bottom line is that this valuable organization depends on donations and committed volunteers.
