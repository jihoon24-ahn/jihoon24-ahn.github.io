✏️ Key Corrections

Tap a correction to see the revision and explanation.

1. Talking about weekend plans
Your wording"Actually we didn't make... we didn't have a plan... we didn't have plans yet, but I will go to my cousin's house."
Minimal correction"Actually, we don't have plans yet, but I will go to my cousin's house."
Natural version"We haven't made any plans yet, but I'm going to my cousin's house tonight."
Why? The plans are still in the future, so use the present: we don't have plans yet, or we haven't made any plans yet. For an arranged visit, I'm going is more natural than I will go.
2. Explaining why you stopped tennis
Your wording"Actually, I had a tennis lesson every weekend, but I canceled... uh, I didn't apply again."
Minimal correction"Actually, I had tennis lessons every weekend, but I didn't apply again."
Natural version"I used to take tennis lessons every weekend, but I didn't sign up again."
Why? Used to shows a past habit that stopped. People sign up for lessons; apply sounds like a job or a university.
3. Saying you know a sport
Your wording"Yes, I heard of it."
Minimal correction"Yes, I've heard of it."
Natural version"Yes, I've heard of it, but I've never played it."
Why? For life experience with no specific time, use the present perfect: I've heard of it. This came up on November 2 too.
4. Describing the pickleball ball
Your wording"Yeah, so how hard you hit, it doesn't go far."
Minimal correction"Yeah, so however hard you hit it, it doesn't go far."
Natural version"No matter how hard you hit it, it doesn't go very far."
Why? No matter how introduces a condition that changes nothing. Hit also needs an object: hit it.
5. Describing a squash shot
Your wording"So, in squash you don't hit a ball very far. You just hit slightly to the wall."
Minimal correction"So, in squash you don't hit the ball very far. You just hit it lightly against the wall."
Natural version"In squash, you don't hit the ball far. You just tap it against the wall."
Why? Slightly means a little; lightly means without much force. A ball hits a surface against the wall, not to it.
6. Describing your first try
Your wording"So I was—at the first time, I was very confused."
Minimal correction"So at first, I was very confused."
Natural version"At first, I was really confused."
Why? At first means at the beginning, before things changed. At the first time is not used in English; the first time works without at.
7. Describing the umpire's decision
Your wording"But the umpire didn't declare something for illegal movement."
Minimal correction"But the umpire didn't declare it an illegal movement."
Natural version"But the umpire didn't call it a foul."
Why? Referees and umpires call a foul. Declare something for is not a natural pattern; use declare it + noun or, better, call it.
8. Planning how to get somewhere
Your wording"I think it's far from my office, so I should prepare how to go to my cousin's house."
Minimal correction"I think it's far from my office, so I should plan how to go to my cousin's house."
Natural version"It's pretty far from my office, so I need to figure out how to get to my cousin's place."
Why? Prepare takes an object such as a meal or a document. Figure out how to get to is the natural chunk for working out a route.

💬 Useful Language

put (someone) off

Meaning
To make someone lose interest in something.

Example
"The confusing rules really put me off squash."

lose your temper

Meaning
To suddenly become very angry.

Example
"The player lost his temper and yelled at the umpire."

scroll through (something)

Meaning
To move down a screen looking at content.

Example
"I was scrolling through Instagram on the subway."

pop up

Meaning
To appear suddenly, especially on a screen.

Example
"A funny squash video popped up in my feed."

get used to (something)

Meaning
To become comfortable with something new.

Example
"It takes time to get used to the light pickleball ball."

sign up (for)

Meaning
To register for a class or activity.

Example
"I didn't sign up for tennis lessons this winter."

head straight home

Meaning
To go home directly without stopping.

Example
"I'm not going out for dinner. I'm heading straight home."

get on with (your day)

Meaning
To continue with what you need to do.

Example
"I'll let you get on with your day."

Why I lost interest in squash
