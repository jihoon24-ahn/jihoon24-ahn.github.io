🚀 Priority Patterns & Suggestions

1. Control articles in noun phrases

Singular countable nouns sometimes appeared without "a" or "an," especially after words such as "interesting" and "big."

Try: Before finishing a noun phrase, quickly ask: "Is this one countable thing?" If yes, add an article.

  • "It is an interesting question."
  • "He was quite a big dog by Korean standards."

2. Use clear time and shift frames

Your shift explanation contained useful detail, but the timeline became hard to follow when several times and policies were packed together.

Try: Use the frame "The first shift runs from X to Y. Then..." and explain one shift per sentence.

  • "The day shift runs from 6 a.m. to 2 p.m."
  • "The night shift ends at 6 a.m., and there is no scheduled overlap."

3. Build questions with "do" and a comparison

Curiosity kept the conversation moving, but some questions used statement word order or an incomplete comparison.

Try: Start with "Do they...?" and finish comparisons with "as...as."

  • "Do they eat as much as humans do?"
  • "Do large dogs need more calories than smaller dogs?"

4. Strengthen verb–noun collocations

A few understandable combinations were not the phrases English speakers normally use, such as "works 24 hours" for a factory and "experience about" a policy.

Try: Memorize the whole chunks "operate 24 hours a day" and "have experience with."

  • "The factory operates 24 hours a day."
  • "I do not have much experience with the shift-work policy."

5. Restart instead of extending a broken sentence

When an idea became complex, you sometimes repeated fragments and changed grammar midway. The meaning was recoverable, but fluency and accuracy dropped.

Try: Say "Let me rephrase that", pause, and restart with a short subject–verb sentence.

  • "Let me rephrase that. Dogs descended from wolves, so raw meat can be natural for them."
  • "Let me put it another way. Feeding stray cats can also increase their population."

✏️ Key Corrections

Tap a correction to see the revision and explanation.

1. Place the time adverb naturally
Your wording"Not yet. Now I'm walking to my office."
Minimal correction"Not yet. I'm walking to my office now."
Natural version"Not yet. I'm on my way to the office now."
Why? "Now" usually sounds more natural after the main phrase here. "I'm on my way" is a common conversational expression for traveling somewhere.
2. Describe continuous operation
Your wording"My company is a semiconductor factory, and it works 24 hours."
Minimal correction"My company has a semiconductor factory, and it works 24 hours a day."
Natural version"My company operates a semiconductor factory that runs 24 hours a day."
Why? A company is not itself a factory, and machines or facilities normally "run" or "operate." Adding "a day" completes the time expression.
3. Explain arrival before a shift
Your wording"They should come to the office about early 30 minutes or an hour before their working time."
Minimal correction"They should come to the office about 30 minutes or an hour before their working time."
Natural version"They have to arrive 30 minutes to an hour before their shift starts."
Why? "About early" combines two incompatible time markers. "Before their shift starts" is clearer and more idiomatic than "before their working time."
4. Use the right preposition with experience
Your wording"I don't have much experience about shift worker policy."
Minimal correction"I don't have much experience with the shift-worker policy."
Natural version"I don't know much about the shift-work policy because I'm an office worker."
Why? We usually say "experience with" a system or activity. "Shift-work policy" is the natural compound noun; the natural version also states your intended limitation directly.
5. Add an article before a singular noun
Your wording"I also think that it is very interesting question."
Minimal correction"I also think that it is a very interesting question."
Natural version"I also thought it raised an interesting question."
Why? Singular countable nouns such as "question" need an article. "Raised an interesting question" is a useful phrase for discussing an article.
6. Describe where you used to live
Your wording"Actually, about five years ago, I lived another small apartment."
Minimal correction"Actually, about five years ago, I lived in another small apartment."
Natural version"About five years ago, I lived in a different apartment complex."
Why? "Live" needs the preposition "in" before a place. "A different apartment complex" clearly introduces the earlier location where the stray cats gathered.
7. Describe a large childhood pet
Your wording"I had a dog, and he was quite big dog in Korean standards."
Minimal correction"I had a dog, and he was quite a big dog by Korean standards."
Natural version"I had a dog who was considered quite large by Korean standards."
Why? "Dog" needs "a," and the fixed comparison phrase is "by Korean standards." "Who was considered" makes the comparison especially smooth.
8. Form a complete comparison question
Your wording"Are they eating like humans? Amount of humans?"
Minimal correction"Do they eat the same amount as humans?"
Natural version"Do they eat as much as humans do?"
Why? Use "do" for a general-habit question. "As much as" creates a complete and natural comparison of food quantity.

💬 Useful Language

on my way to

Traveling toward a place. "I'm on my way to the office."

run 24 hours a day

Operate continuously. "The factory runs 24 hours a day."

a seamless handoff

A smooth transfer with no interruption. "An overlap allows a seamless handoff."

if it were me

Used to give a personal view about someone else's situation. "If it were me, I would set a spending limit."

spoil a pet

Give a pet excessive treats, attention, or luxuries. "My mother loves to spoil her cats."

by Korean standards

Compared with what is typical in Korea. "The dog was large by Korean standards."

raise an interesting question

Introduce an issue worth discussing. "The article raises an interesting question about pet ownership."

implement a policy

Put an official plan or rule into practice. "The city could implement a trap-neuter-return policy."

A Balanced Approach to Stray Cats

In my previous apartment complex, one resident fed stray cats every morning. I understand that she wanted to help them, but many cats began gathering near the building and around my car. Feeding them without managing the population can create problems because the cats reproduce and may affect the local ecosystem. I think local governments should implement a trap-neuter-return policy. Volunteers could feed the cats only after they have been treated, and the city could provide financial support. This approach would protect the animals while also reducing complaints from residents. It seems like a more balanced and responsible solution.
