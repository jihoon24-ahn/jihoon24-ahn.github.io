  • “I sent the emissions report to the external auditors.”

2. Match verb tense to time and experience

Use the simple past for a finished event and the present perfect for experience or a situation connected to now.

  • “My wife has already visited Spain.”
  • “I haven’t been to Ashgabat yet.”

3. Control passive and causative structures

When someone receives an action, English often needs be + past participle or have + object + past participle.

  • “He was appointed ambassador several months ago.”
  • “She was reluctant to have her picture taken.”

4. Break long stories into complete sentences

Long explanations sometimes contained repeated starts, unclear pronouns, or missing verbs. Complete the event before adding background or interpretation.

🚀 Suggestions to Improve

Store prepositions inside useful work and travel chunks

Practice the whole phrase rather than selecting the preposition during the conversation.

Example: “I sent the report to the auditors, and I asked Catherine about her trip to Turkmenistan.”

Use a finished-time versus life-experience check

Choose the simple past with a finished time and the present perfect with already, yet, or general experience.

Example: “Catherine went there ten years ago, but I haven’t been there yet.”

Ask who receives the action

Before speaking, identify whether the subject performs the action or receives it. This will guide active, passive, or causative form.

Example: “The government appointed him, so he was appointed ambassador. A photographer took the photo, so she had her picture taken.”

Use a three-sentence story reset

When a story becomes tangled, restart with situation, event, and result. Add details only after those three sentences are complete.

Example: “We went on a field trip. One student refused the group photo, and another student spoke sharply. Later, I explained that her reaction was not typical in Korea.”

Your wording“A few hours ago, I sent my report of my company directories. Greenhouse gas emissions report.”

Natural version“A few hours ago, I sent my company’s greenhouse-gas emissions report to the external auditors.”

3. Use the causative form for a photograph
Your wording“One student was reluctant to take a picture.”
Minimal correction“One student was reluctant to have her picture taken.”
Natural version“One student did not want to be included in the group photo.”
Why? Take a picture means operate the camera. Because someone else would photograph the student, use have her picture taken or the passive idea be included in the photo.

Natural version“I didn’t know her well, and we weren’t friends.”
Why? After a friend of, use the independent possessive pronoun hers. The natural version states the degree of familiarity more directly.

Minimal correction“I apologized for her behavior.”
Natural version“I apologized on her behalf and explained that her reaction was not typical.”
Why? Because the event happened in the past, use apologized. On her behalf means you offered the apology for another person.

Minimal correction“I’m glad that I gave you the idea for the session.”
Natural version“I’m glad our earlier conversations became the spark for your Advice Club.”
Why? A person is not usually the hint of a class. Give someone the idea is the minimal meaning, while be the spark for naturally describes inspiring something new.
7. Introduce the regional summit
Your wording“In this week, a Central Asia and Korea summit is being held.”
Minimal correction“This week, a Central Asia–Korea summit is being held.”
Natural version“A summit between Korea and Central Asian countries is taking place this week.”

8. Use the passive form with “appoint”
Your wording“He appointed as a ambassador.”
Minimal correction“He was appointed as an ambassador.”
Natural version“He was appointed ambassador about four or five months ago.”
Why? The person receives the appointment, so use was appointed. Use an before a vowel sound; in the natural version, the title can follow appointed without an article.
9. Use the present perfect for a prior visit
Your wording“My wife doesn’t like to go to Spain because she already visited there.”
Minimal correction“My wife doesn’t want to go to Spain because she has already visited.”
Natural version“My wife doesn’t want to return to Spain because she’s already been there.”
Why? Use want to go for preference about a future trip. The present perfect with already connects her past visit to her current decision.
10. Ask about a past trip
Your wording“How about your trip in Turkmenistan?”
Minimal correction“How about your trip to Turkmenistan?”
Natural version“What was your trip to Turkmenistan like?”
Why? Use to for a trip’s destination. What was…like? naturally invites someone to describe an experience rather than answer yes or no.

💬 Useful Language

turnaround time

The time needed to complete and return work. “The auditors’ turnaround time may be about a week.”

a fire to put out

An urgent problem that requires immediate action. “The report did not reveal a fire that management had to put out.”

have my picture taken

Be photographed by someone else.

apologize on someone’s behalf

Offer an apology for another person. “I apologized on her behalf.”

a venting session

A conversation in which someone expresses frustration or difficult feelings.

take it out on someone

Treat someone badly because you are upset about something else. “She was stressed at work and took it out on me.”

be the spark for something

Provide the initial idea or energy that starts something.

a token amount

A deliberately small payment made mainly for symbolic reasons.
