🚀 Priority Patterns & Suggestions

1. Learn verbs with their prepositions

Verbs often came with the wrong small word: replace … to, invest time to, save money to fire, limit using of. In class, you also wondered about confused about; that one was right for a topic. Confused with means mixing up two things.

Try: Store each verb as a verb + preposition chunk: replace A with B, spend time on, save money by -ing, confused about.

  • "Some companies want to replace workers with AI."
  • "I want to spend more time on valuable work."

2. Check the verb form after the subject

Verb forms slipped after singular subjects and modals: AI think, He think, it spend, we will not able to.

Try: Use two quick checks: he / she / it / AI + -s, and will be able to (never will able to).

  • "AI thinks for us, so we think less."
  • "Someday, we won't be able to think without AI."

3. Build comparatives correctly

You added more to words that already change: more easier, more busy. You also asked Daryl to explain more easily, where more simply fits better.

Try: Short adjectives take -er (easier, busier); longer ones take more (more useful). Never use both.

  • "It's a beginner class, so it's easier than learning Python."
  • "For a while I'll be busier, but AI is more useful in the long run."

4. Use the present continuous for today's plans

For plans already arranged, you used will or the simple present: Today I will work at home, that's why I take a class today. Last time it was she will go on a boat trip.

Try: If the plan is already set, say I'm + -ing with a time word. Save will for predictions and quick decisions.

  • "I'm working from home today because I have a Zoom class."
  • "I'm taking an AI course this afternoon."

5. Describe the word you can't find

When a word was missing, you guessed close words: fresh men, newly grad, massive fire, very dangerous. Guesses can change the meaning.

Try: Use a describe-it frame: say what the thing is or does, then let the listener supply the word.

  • "I mean people who just finished university and started their first job."
  • "What's the word for when a company fires thousands of people at once?"

✏️ Key Corrections

Tap a correction to see the revision and explanation.

1. Saying where you're working today
Your wording"Today I will work at home because I have a class."
Minimal correction"Today I'm working at home because I have a class."
Natural version"I'm working from home today because I have a Zoom class."
Why? For a plan already arranged, use the present continuous. Work from home is the standard phrase for remote work.
2. Describing the class level
Your wording"I think a more easier one, you know?"
Minimal correction"I think an easier one."
Natural version"I think it's an easier one, more of a beginner class."
Why? Easier already means more easy, so more easier doubles it. The article changes to an before a vowel sound.
3. Reporting one side of the debate
Your wording"We should limit using of AI because AI think and we don't think. So we, someday we will not able to think anymore without AI."
Minimal correction"We should limit our use of AI because AI thinks and we don't think. Someday we will not be able to think anymore without AI."
Natural version"We should use AI less, because when AI thinks for us, we stop thinking. Someday, we won't be able to think without it."
Why? Say limit our use of AI or limit using AI, not using of. AI is singular, so thinks. Will needs be able to.
4. Retelling a friend's comparison
Your wording"He told me that I, he looks... Ah, he looks... He think about AI, it's similar to GPS system about 30 years ago."
Minimal correction"He told me that he thinks AI is similar to the GPS system about 30 years ago."
Natural version"He thinks AI today is like GPS was 30 years ago."
Why? Choose one subject and one verb: he thinks. Looks means appearance. A is like B was compares two things at different times neatly.
5. Explaining the reason for layoffs
Your wording"And they want to save some money to fire employees."
Minimal correction"And they want to save some money by firing employees."
Natural version"They want to cut jobs to free up money for data centers."
Why? To fire makes firing the goal; by firing shows the method. Cut jobs and free up money are natural business chunks.
6. Asking someone to rephrase
Your wording"Excuse me, could you say once again and explain more easily?"
Minimal correction"Excuse me, could you say that again and explain it more simply?"
Natural version"Sorry, could you rephrase that more simply?"
Why? Say needs an object: say that again. People explain simply, not easily. Could you rephrase that? is a polite, ready-made request.
7. Describing the time cost of a task
Your wording"It spend, it spend a lot of time, but if I make a automated program, I will save a lot of time, and then I will spend, I will imp- ah, invest time other to other jobs."
Minimal correction"It takes a lot of time, but if I make an automated program, I will save a lot of time, and then I will invest that time in other jobs."
Natural version"It takes up a lot of time. If I automate it, I can spend that time on more valuable work."
Why? A task takes time; people spend time. Use an before automated, and invest or spend time in or on something, not to.
8. Talking about being busy
Your wording"For a moment, I will be more busy, but I hope I will I will free..."
Minimal correction"For a while, I will be busier, but I hope I will be free."
Natural version"I'll be busier for a while, but hopefully I'll have more free time eventually."
Why? For a moment means a few seconds; for a while means some time. Busy becomes busier, and free is an adjective, so it needs be.

💬 Useful Language

tedious

Meaning
Boring because it is long and repetitive.

Example
"Downloading every utility bill by hand is tedious."

free up (my schedule)

Meaning
To create free time by removing tasks.

Example
"Automating this report will free up my schedule."

lay off / a mass layoff

Meaning
To end workers' jobs, often to cut costs; a large wave of job cuts.

Example
"The company laid off 50,000 employees in a mass layoff."

recent graduates (recent grads)

Meaning
People who finished university not long ago.

Example
"Entry-level jobs for recent grads are shrinking because of AI."

in high demand

Meaning
Wanted by many people or companies.

Example
"People who can work without AI may be in high demand again."

backtrack

Meaning
To reverse a decision or statement.

Example
"Several companies backtracked and started hiring again."

set yourself apart

Meaning
To make yourself different from and better than others.

Example
"Building my own automation tool could set me apart from my coworkers."

to an extent

Meaning
Partly, but not completely.

Example
"To an extent, AI helps us compete, but it can also make us lazy thinkers."
