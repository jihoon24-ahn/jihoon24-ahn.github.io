🚀 Priority Patterns & Suggestions

1. Finish a sentence before adding detail

The projector story and audit explanation contain repeated starts such as "so," "at that time," and "I…". Your ideas are relevant, but too many unfinished clauses make their sequence harder to follow.

Try: Use one idea per sentence. When a sentence gets tangled, say "Let me put it this way" and restart with a subject and verb.

  • "I submitted the documents. Now I’m waiting for feedback."
  • "I gave a presentation. Afterward, some classmates approached me."

2. Set the timeline before telling a story

You clarified this Monday versus last Monday and shifted tense while describing first meetings and graduate school. A clear time marker helps listeners follow the whole story.

Try: Start with when + past event, then keep completed actions in the past. Separate your current situation with now.

  • "Earlier this week, I finished the main audit tasks."
  • "When I first arrived in D.C., some classmates asked me about Korean skincare."

3. Learn the verb together with its pattern

"Apply more documents" and "made me connecting more people" show that the intended meaning is clear but the verb or its following form needs adjustment.

Try: Practice complete chunks: submit documents, help someone connect with people, and be responsible for reporting. With make + person, use a base verb.

  • "The auditors may ask me to submit additional documents."
  • "The film helped me connect with more classmates."

4. Use fixed phrases for first meetings and business ideas

Expressions such as "at the first time" and "one of the promising business" become easier when you retrieve the whole phrase instead of assembling each word during speech.

Try: Rehearse for the first time, a promising business idea, and one of the + plural noun. Use for for a first occurrence.

  • "We met for the first time in an English class."
  • "Selling Korean skincare could be one of the promising business opportunities in that market."

5. Separate predictions from firm conclusions

You moved between uncertainty about audit requests and strong claims about Chinese chipmakers. Phrases such as "they must give me" can sound more certain than your evidence allows.

Try: Use I expect or they may for predictions. For market opinions, give a condition, a likely effect, and one limitation.

  • "I expect the auditors to request some changes, but I don’t know exactly what they’ll ask for."
  • "If Chinese chipmakers increase supply, prices could fall, although product quality will also matter."

✏️ Key Corrections

Tap a correction to see the revision and explanation.

1. Place the negative naturally
Your wording"I hope today will be not very busy."
Minimal correction"I hope today will not be very busy."
Natural version"I hope today won’t be too busy."
Why? Put not after the modal will and before be. The contraction won’t and the phrase too busy make the same hope sound more conversational.
2. Name the responsibility precisely
Your wording"I'm responsible for greenhouse gas for my factory."
Minimal correction"I'm responsible for greenhouse gas reporting for my factory."
Natural version"I handle greenhouse gas reporting at my factory."
Why? The intended task is document reporting, not responsibility for the gas itself. Adding reporting identifies the work; handle reporting is a concise way to describe that role.
3. Submit documents; apply for permission
Your wording"You should apply more documents."
Minimal correction"You should submit more documents."
Natural version"Please submit some additional documents."
Why? Use submit when sending documents for review. Apply for usually introduces something you want, such as a permit. The natural version uses a clear, polite request an auditor might make.
4. Describe a likely request
Your wording"They must give me some feedback."
Minimal correction"They will probably give me some feedback."
Natural version"I expect the auditors to send me some feedback."
Why? In this context, you are predicting feedback rather than stating an obligation. Must can imply duty or strong certainty; probably and I expect better express your expectation.
5. People ask questions
Your wording"Some students came to me and they answered, "Have you ever seen, watched K-Pop Demon Hunters?""
Minimal correction"Some students came to me and they asked, "Have you ever seen K-Pop Demon Hunters?""
Natural version"Afterward, some classmates came up to me and asked, "Have you seen K-Pop Demon Hunters?""
Why? The classmates initiated a question, so use asked, not answered. Choose one verb, seen or watched. Came up to me naturally describes approaching someone to start a conversation.
6. Connect with people
Your wording"K-Pop Demon Hunters made me connecting more people."
Minimal correction"K-Pop Demon Hunters made me connect with more people."
Natural version"K-Pop Demon Hunters helped me connect with more people."
Why? Use make + person + base verb, not connecting, and connect with for social relationships. Helped me is more natural here because the film provided an opportunity rather than forcing an action.
7. Use the first-meeting phrase
Your wording"We meet at the first time."
Minimal correction"We met for the first time."
Natural version"We had only just met, so the question felt a little awkward."
Why? Use the past tense met for this completed encounter and the fixed phrase for the first time. The natural version links the new acquaintance to the awkwardness you described.
8. Use a plural noun after one of
Your wording"It is one of the promising business."
Minimal correction"It is one of the promising businesses."
Natural version"Selling Korean skincare in D.C. seemed like a promising business idea."
Why? One of the selects one member of a group, so the count noun must be plural: businesses. In your story, business idea is more precise because you were considering a possibility; seemed keeps the account in the past.
