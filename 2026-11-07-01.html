🚀 Priority Patterns & Suggestions

1. Learn time and verb prepositions as chunks

Prepositions slipped with months and verbs: starts on March, negotiate them, in front of the public, enough to me.

Try: Review the November 2 rule and add verb chunks: in March, negotiate with, in front of an audience, enough for me.

  • "In Korea, the school year starts in March."
  • "You have to negotiate with the driver before you go."

2. Don't mix be with another verb

Be was added before main verbs: I am still think, semester is start. A main verb was also dropped: she wants to a grocery store.

Try: Use one check: be + adjective / -ing, or a main verb alone, never both.

  • "I still think 15 minutes isn't enough."
  • "The second semester starts in September, and she wants to go to the store."

3. Report what someone said or asked

Reported speech mixed direct and indirect forms: she often told me that I feel that your English is getting rusty, she asked me… What do you want to go?

Try: Change the pronouns to the reporter's view: she tells me (that) my English…, she asked me where I wanted to go. Or quote directly: She says, "Your English is getting rusty."

  • "My teacher often tells me that my English is getting rusty."
  • "My wife asked me where I wanted to go during my visit."

4. Describe routines in the simple present

Habits came out in the past or with the wrong time phrase: after the meeting, I made an English report every time, At the time, I'm walking to my office.

Try: For anything you do regularly, use the simple present + a frequency word: every day, usually, after every class.

  • "After every class, I make an English report and review it."
  • "Every morning, I walk to my office and talk to my teacher on the phone."

5. Explain a system step by step

Explaining the Korean semester system, the sentence looped: Spring semester is first semester, and fourth semester is the second semester, and fourth semester is start on March… Cheryl had to stop you to check.

Try: Use a sequence frame: one short sentence per step, linked with first, then, and after that.

  • "In Korea, the school year has two semesters. The first one starts in March."
  • "Then there's a summer break, and after that, the second semester starts in September."

✏️ Key Corrections

Tap a correction to see the revision and explanation.

1. Referring to something you said before
Your wording"I can make an English report as I might mentioned before."
Minimal correction"I can make an English report, as I might have mentioned before."
Natural version"I make an English report, as I may have mentioned before."
Why? After might or may, a past action needs have + past participle: may have mentioned. This is a polite way to avoid repeating yourself.
2. Explaining why you practice while walking
Your wording"It's a little bit embarrassed to speak in front of many Koreans."
Minimal correction"It's a little bit embarrassing to speak in front of many Koreans."
Natural version"It's a little embarrassing to speak English in front of other Koreans."
Why? -ing describes the situation (it's embarrassing); -ed describes your feeling (I'm embarrassed). With it's, use embarrassing.
3. Saying your practice time isn't enough
Your wording"Well, I'm still, I am still think every 15 minutes English speaking is not enough to me to… enough for me to… maintain my English skills."
Minimal correction"Well, I still think 15 minutes of English speaking every day is not enough for me to maintain my English skills."
Natural version"I still think 15 minutes of English a day isn't enough to keep my skills up."
Why? I am still think mixes be with a verb. Use 15 minutes of English a day, not every 15 minutes, which means repeatedly every quarter hour. Your fix to enough for me was right.
4. Reporting what your teacher says
Your wording"She often told me that I feel that your English is getting rusty."
Minimal correction"She often tells me that she feels that my English is getting rusty."
Natural version"She often tells me my English is getting rusty."
Why? She still says it, so use tells. In reported speech, change the pronouns: I → she, your → my.
5. Explaining a lost language
Your wording"She couldn't speak Chinese very well fluently because she forgot to speak."
Minimal correction"She couldn't speak Chinese very fluently because she forgot how to speak it."
Natural version"She can't speak Chinese fluently anymore because she's forgotten a lot of it."
Why? Forgot to speak means she didn't remember to say something. For a lost skill, use forgot how to or has forgotten. Choose very well or fluently, not both.
6. Explaining the Korean school year
Your wording"Academic year starts on March."
Minimal correction"The academic year starts in March."
Natural version"In Korea, the school year starts in March."
Why? Use in with months and on with dates. Academic year also needs the.
7. Explaining how to hire a driver
Your wording"Ah, so before the signing, you have to negotiate them."
Minimal correction"So before signing, you have to negotiate with them."
Natural version"Before you agree on anything, you have to negotiate the price with them."
Why? You negotiate with a person and negotiate a price. Before the signing sounds formal; before you sign or before you agree is natural.
8. Asking about a destination
Your wording"What do you want to go?"
Minimal correction"Where do you want to go?"
Natural version"Where would you like to go?"
Why? Go needs a place, so the question word is where. Use what only with a verb that takes an object: What do you want to see?

Meaning
Becoming weaker because you don't use a skill often.

Example
"I don't use English much at work, so it's getting rusty."

Meaning
