🚀 Priority Patterns & Suggestions

1. Learn place and time prepositions as chunks

Prepositions were missing or wrong in several places: go Europe, went out the restaurant, in weekends, and on the commute.

Try: Memorize the verb and preposition together as one unit: go to, get out of, on weekends, at the meeting.

  • "I usually visit my parents on weekends."
  • "They didn't like the menu, so they left the restaurant and went to a cheaper place."

2. Choose a, the, or no article

Articles were added or dropped: the Halloween, in meeting, go to restaurant, more common place, and a woman for someone already introduced.

Try: Use three quick checks: no article for holidays, a for the first mention, and the for someone or something already mentioned.

  • "Did you enjoy Halloween?" (no article)
  • "A couple went to a restaurant. The woman didn't like the menu."

3. Tell short stories with clear characters

In the Instagram story, the characters shifted between two people, a man, they, and a woman, and the main point came out in pieces.

Try: Use a story frame: introduce the people once, say what happened, then give the result.

  • "I saw a video about a couple at a fancy restaurant."
  • "The man ordered something special, but the woman didn't like it, so in the end, they went somewhere cheaper."

4. Talk about future plans with -ing

Your ChatGPT note was right: the present continuous describes a fixed plan or arrangement, with no will needed. Going to works for plans too, and will is for quick decisions or predictions.

Try: Add a future time word to the -ing form so the listener knows it is a plan: soon, tonight, tomorrow, next week.

  • "I'm leaving soon, so let's talk later."
  • "I'm flying to Europe tomorrow, and I'll call you when I land."

5. Pick the precise word

Some words were close but not right: Germany (the country) for German (the language), gorgeous for fancy, common for casual, and menu for dish.

Try: Learn word pairs that Korean speakers often mix up, and practice them in a sentence, not alone.

  • "English borrows many words from French and German."
  • "He ordered a fancy dish, but she wanted a more casual place."

✏️ Key Corrections

Tap a correction to see the revision and explanation.

1. Asking about a holiday
Your wording"And did you enjoy the Halloween?"
Minimal correction"And did you enjoy Halloween?"
Natural version"Did you do anything fun for Halloween?"
Why? Holidays such as Halloween, Christmas, and Chuseok take no article. Do anything fun for a holiday is a common, friendly way to ask.
2. Explaining your weekend routine
Your wording"You know, my wife is abroad, so she is working in another country, so I usually go to my parents' home on weekends."
Minimal correction"My wife is working abroad, so I usually go to my parents' home on weekends."
Natural version"My wife works abroad, so I usually spend weekends at my parents' place."
Why? Is abroad and working in another country repeat the same idea, and two so clauses make a chain. One cause and one result is clearer. Spend weekends at is a natural chunk.
3. Asking about two prepositions
Your wording"What's the difference between in meeting, in meeting, and at a meeting?"
Minimal correction"What's the difference between in a meeting and at a meeting?"
Natural version"What's the difference between "in a meeting" and "at a meeting"?"
Why? Meeting is a countable noun, so it needs a: in a meeting. Saying the two phrases once each, in parallel, makes the question easier to follow.
4. Describing what ChatGPT taught you
Your wording"ChatGPT taught me that ing verb can imply you will something in the future."
Minimal correction"ChatGPT taught me that an -ing verb can show that you will do something in the future."
Natural version"ChatGPT told me the present continuous can describe future plans."
Why? Ing verb needs an article, and will something is missing the verb do. Present continuous is the grammar name to remember, and describe future plans states the idea in fewer words.
5. Going to a place
Your wording"I'm going to go Europe tomorrow."
Minimal correction"I'm going to go to Europe tomorrow."
Natural version"I'm going to Europe tomorrow."
Why? Go needs to before a place. Going to go to is correct but heavy; I'm going to Europe tomorrow is shorter and is the present continuous for a plan.
6. Saying expressions are similar
Your wording"There are so many confusing expressions, for they have a slight difference."
Minimal correction"There are so many confusing expressions because they have only a slight difference."
Natural version"A lot of expressions are confusing because they're only slightly different, like "used to" and "get used to.""
Why? For meaning because sounds old-fashioned in speech; use because. Only slightly different is the natural way to describe a small difference.
7. Starting a story about a video
Your wording"In the video, two people go to restaurant, and a man, they went, and a man ordered something special and gorgeous."
Minimal correction"In the video, two people went to a restaurant, and the man ordered something special and fancy."
Natural version"In the video, a couple went to a fancy restaurant, and the man ordered something special."
Why? Keep the story in the past: went, not go. Restaurant needs a, and the man is the man once the two people are introduced. Gorgeous describes beauty; fancy describes an expensive, high-end place or meal.
8. Saying you might know a word
Your wording"I think I heard of it, but I don't know what it is."
Minimal correction"I think I've heard of it, but I don't know what it means."
Natural version"It sounds familiar, but I'm not sure what it means."
Why? For life experience without a specific time, use the present perfect: I've heard of it. Ask what a word means, not what it is. It sounds familiar is a handy alternative.

💬 Useful Language

the year flew by

Meaning
The year passed very quickly.

Example
"I can't believe it's already November. This year flew by."

head to (a place)

Meaning
To go toward a place.

Example
"I'm heading to my office right now."

in a meeting / at the meeting

Meaning
In a meeting means taking part; at the meeting describes your location.

Example
"Sorry, I can't talk now. I'm in a meeting."

à la carte

Meaning
Ordered as separate items, each with its own price, not as a set meal.

Example
"We skipped the set menu and ordered à la carte."

al fresco

Meaning
Outdoors, usually about eating.

Example
"The weather was nice, so we ate al fresco."

bougie

Meaning
Casual slang for something fancy or trying to look expensive.

Example
"That restaurant is a little too bougie for a weekday lunch."

a dish

Meaning
A particular food prepared as part of a meal.

Example
"He ordered a French dish I had never heard of."

wrap up

Meaning
To finish something, such as a meeting or class.

Example
"We have to wrap up here for today."
