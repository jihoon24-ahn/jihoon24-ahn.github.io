🚀 Priority Patterns & Suggestions

1. Keep the story in one timeline

When you told past stories, the tense often drifted into the present: after I visit, I like them when I was in DC, and everyone is not good. This makes it hard to tell when things happened.

Try: Start with a past time marker such as when I was in DC or when I first joined, then check that every verb in the story is past until you return to now.

  • "When I was in DC, I saw a lot of men with beards, and I really liked the look."
  • "When I first joined the army, I got a terrible cold. These days, I stay healthy most of the time."

2. Choose the exact word for the situation

Several sentences used a word that is close but not correct: cut their face for shave, knives for razors, go to army for join the army, and work at the army for serve in the army.

Try: Learn the topic as fixed chunks, not single words. For grooming and the military, memorize the whole phrase and reuse it exactly.

  • "Most Korean men like to be clean-shaven, but I'm thinking about growing a beard."
  • "Every Korean man has to serve in the military, and many people join the army in their early twenties."

3. Restart once, cleanly

Many sentences contained repeated starts, such as I I I, it was it was, and more safe than safer than. Repetition is normal, but frequent restarts make the listener wait for your main point.

Try: When a sentence goes wrong, stop, pause briefly, and use a restart chunk. Then say the full sentence once from the beginning.

  • "Let me say that again. An electric razor is safer than a regular razor."
  • "What I mean is, it wasn't just me. Everyone got sick."

4. Organize your reasons

When Daryl asked why Korean office workers rarely have beards, you had a strong idea, military service, but it came out slowly in pieces. A simple frame would make the same idea sound clear and confident.

Try: Use a reason frame: state the main reason first, add one example, then finish with a short conclusion.

  • "I think the main reason is military service. Soldiers have to shave every day, so it becomes a habit."
  • "That's one of the reasons why many young Koreans don't stay in the army."

5. Check articles, plurals, and agreement

Small grammar errors appeared repeatedly: a electric razor, one and a half year, one of the reason, and every American soldiers. You self-corrected the last one, which shows you know the rule.

Try: Remember three quick rules: an before a vowel sound, one of the + plural noun, and every + singular noun.

  • "I got an electric razor, and it's one of the best presents I've ever received."
  • "Every soldier has to shave, and service now lasts one and a half years."

✏️ Key Corrections

Tap a correction to see the revision and explanation.

1. Describing a shaving accident
Your wording"I cut my face during, uh, when I, shaving."
Minimal correction"I cut my face when I was shaving."
Natural version"I cut myself shaving this morning."
Why? During must be followed by a noun, not a clause, so during when I shaving doesn't work. When I was shaving fixes the grammar. Native speakers usually say cut myself shaving or nicked myself shaving, with no extra connector.
2. Saying you are not used to something yet
Your wording"Yeah, but I'm getting used to it, so I cut my face this morning."
Minimal correction"Yeah, but I'm still getting used to it, so I cut my face this morning."
Natural version"I'm still getting used to it, so I nicked myself this morning."
Why? Without still, the sentence sounds like you are almost comfortable, which doesn't explain the cut. Still getting used to it shows the process is unfinished, so the cause and result connect logically.
3. Comparing two kinds of razor
Your wording"I think electric laser is more safe than safer than other knives."
Minimal correction"I think an electric razor is safer than other razors."
Natural version"I think an electric razor is safer than a regular blade razor."
Why? Use safer, not more safe, for a one-syllable adjective. Knives are for cutting food; for shaving, say razor or blade. The word laser may be a transcription error, but practice a clear r in razor.
4. Talking about Korean shaving habits
Your wording"In Korea, many men want to cut their face very clean, and we shave their face."
Minimal correction"In Korea, many men want to shave their faces very clean."
Natural version"In Korea, most men like to be clean-shaven."
Why? Cut your face means injuring your skin, so it sounds like an accident. The pronouns also changed from we to their. Be clean-shaven is the natural, compact phrase for this idea.
5. Explaining a cultural discovery
Your wording"I was born in here and I grew up here, so I didn't realize it was common, and now, after I visit the United States, and I saw many people, so now I want to find the reason why."
Minimal correction"I was born here and grew up here, so I didn't realize it was unusual. After I visited the United States and saw many people with beards, I wanted to find the reason why."
Natural version"I was born and raised here, so I never noticed it. But after visiting the U.S. and seeing so many men with beards, I started wondering why."
Why? Born in here should be born here, and visit should be past: visited. I saw many people is incomplete; add with beards. Born and raised and I started wondering why are smooth, reusable chunks.
6. Describing a change in length
Your wording"It has been shortened by one and a half year."
Minimal correction"It has been shortened to one and a half years."
Natural version"Mine was two years, but now it's been cut to 18 months."
Why? By shows the amount of change, so shortened by one and a half years would mean only six months remain. To shows the final result. Also use the plural: one and a half years. Daryl modeled this correction during class.
7. Saying everyone got sick
Your wording"And it does not only me. Everyone was hurt. Everyone was affected, and everyone is not good. I was not good, and everyone was sick."
Minimal correction"And it was not only me. Everyone was affected, and everyone was sick."
Natural version"It wasn't just me. The whole unit got sick."
Why? It does not only me has no main verb meaning; use it wasn't only me. Hurt usually means injured, not sick. Keep the past tense throughout. It wasn't just me is a short, natural way to say others shared the experience.
8. Explaining why soldiers leave the army
Your wording"That's one of the reason why many Koreans don't want to work at the army. After the army experience, we don't have a workplace after the army."
Minimal correction"That's one of the reasons why many Koreans don't want to stay in the army. After military service, we don't have a workplace to go to."
Natural version"That's one of the reasons many Koreans don't make a career of the military. There aren't many jobs that use that experience afterward."
Why? Use one of the reasons with a plural noun, and say in the army, not at the army. Repeating after the army twice sounds unplanned. Make a career of the military expresses staying long-term more precisely.

"I was in a hurry this morning, and I cut myself shaving."

Still becoming comfortable with something new.

Meaning

Example

🗣️ Model Answer

Why are most Korean men clean-shaven?

That's a good question. I was born and raised in Korea, so I never really noticed it until I visited the U.S. and saw so many men with beards. Now I think one of the main reasons is military service. Most Korean men have to serve in the army, and soldiers have to be clean-shaven every day. After two years, shaving just becomes a habit. Also, Korean office culture expects people to look clean-cut and presentable, and nobody wants to stand out. So for most men, being clean-shaven is simply the way it is. Maybe I'll try growing a beard someday, though.
