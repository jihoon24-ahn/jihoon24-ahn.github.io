✏️ Key Corrections

Tap a correction to see the revision and explanation.

1. Comparing two Thanksgivings
Your wording"I think Korean Thanksgiving celebrates before the harvest. And I think American Thanksgiving break celebrates after harvest."
Minimal correction"I think Korean Thanksgiving is celebrated before the harvest. And I think American Thanksgiving is celebrated after the harvest."
Natural version"Korean Thanksgiving comes before the harvest, but American Thanksgiving comes after it."
Why? People celebrate; a holiday doesn't. Use the passive is celebrated, or say the holiday comes before or after. Harvest needs the here.
2. Starting the Thanksgiving story
Your wording"A long time ago, but not very long time—maybe not that long ago—about 200 years or 300 years. So, European ancestors came to America."
Minimal correction"About 200 or 300 years ago, European settlers came to America."
Natural version"A few hundred years ago, settlers from Europe came to America."
Why? Use ago once, after the number. Ancestors are your own family from long ago; people who move to a new land are settlers.
3. Ending a story
Your wording"After harvest, they shared food, dishes, and they happily lived ever after."
Minimal correction"After the harvest, they shared food, and they lived happily ever after."
Natural version"After the harvest, they shared a big meal and lived happily ever after."
Why? Lived happily ever after is a fixed phrase from fairy tales; the word order can't change. Harvest needs the.
4. Reporting what people told you
Your wording"Many people told me the same thing. So it was great, it was a traditional food in America, but you don't need to try it."
Minimal correction"Many people told me the same thing: it was a traditional food in America, but I didn't need to try it."
Natural version"Everyone told me the same thing: it's a classic American dish, but you don't really need to try it."
Why? When you report someone's words, shift the pronoun to your point of view: you don't need → I didn't need. Or quote them directly.
5. Asking whether you mentioned something
Your wording"Did I say before New York City bagel is really, really expensive in South Korea?"
Minimal correction"Did I tell you before that a New York City bagel is really expensive in South Korea?"
Natural version"Did I ever tell you that New York bagels are super expensive in Korea?"
Why? Say doesn't take a person; tell does: Did I tell you that…? A general statement about bagels sounds best in the plural.
6. Giving a mild opinion
Your wording"It was delicious, but I thought it was not that very, very special."
Minimal correction"It was delicious, but I thought it was not that special."
Natural version"It was good, but I didn't think it was anything special."
Why? Put the negative on think: I didn't think it was…. That and very both intensify, so use one.
7. Sharing a bagel
Your wording"My wife and I divided them and we ate it one by one."
Minimal correction"My wife and I divided it, and we each ate one half."
Natural version"My wife and I split it and each had half."
Why? One by one means one after another. For sharing, use split it and each had half. Keep the pronoun singular: one bagel, it.
8. Summing up the experience
Your wording"Environment and experience was great, but food was not great."
Minimal correction"The environment and experience were great, but the food was not great."
Natural version"The atmosphere and the experience were great, but the food wasn't."
Why? Two subjects joined by and take were. Specific things need the. Atmosphere is the natural word for how a place feels, and wasn't avoids repeating great.

Meaning
To search for information.

Example
"I looked up a turkey recipe and gave up immediately."

give up

Meaning
To stop trying.

Example
"It needed eight hours in the oven, so I gave up."

Meaning

Example
"I agree wholeheartedly: turkey is too dry."

Meaning

Example

Meaning
