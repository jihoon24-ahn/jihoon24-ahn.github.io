🚀 Priority Patterns & Suggestions

1. Finish the main sentence first

The job-application story, apartment description, and voting explanation contain repeated restarts. Your listener can often recover the point, but has to help connect the pieces.

Try: Use point → detail → result. If you lose the sentence, pause and say "Let me start again." Restart with a clear subject + verb.

  • "I did not get an interview. I had hoped to reach that stage, even if I did not get the job."
  • "The building used door signs. The signs showed children which apartments were participating."

2. Keep the story on one timeline

The housewarming story and candy story shift between present and past forms. You also tried several forms of hide before continuing.

Try: Begin with a time marker and keep completed actions in the past tense. Prepare a three-sentence situation → action → outcome story before adding quoted speech.

  • "Last night, I visited a colleague who had recently moved into a new home."
  • "His son collected a lot of candy, hid it in his room, and ate it over the following months."

3. Build questions from a fixed frame

You successfully asked follow-up questions, but the Halloween questions, turnip question, and candy question show unstable auxiliary verbs and noun forms. You did repair does we to do we yourself.

Try: Choose a frame before adding details: What is + singular noun? or How much + uncountable noun + did + subject + base verb? Treat candy as a total amount when asking about a Halloween collection.

  • "What is a turnip? Is it similar to a radish?"
  • "How much candy did your sons get last Halloween?"

4. Retrieve a phrase instead of one word

You searched for base, polling place, and supporters, and Cheryl supplied several expressions. A useful success: she prompted looking forward to, and you reused it independently later in the conversation.

Try: Repeat new vocabulary as a complete reusable chunk, then use it in a second example. If a word is missing, describe its function and ask "What do you call that?"

  • "I hoped to get to the interview stage, but my application was unsuccessful."
  • "You show your ID at the polling place, and the staff check your voting district."

5. Use ready-made time and place phrases

You hesitated over in/on while describing door signs and church grounds, and used ago to relate voting to election day. These small words become easier as part of a phrase.

Try: Practise on the door, in the churchyard, and a month before election day. Use ago for time measured backward from now; use before to connect two events.

  • "Residents put a sticker on the door to show they were participating."
  • "You can vote a month before election day, but you might change your mind afterward."

✏️ Key Corrections

Tap a correction to see the revision and explanation.

1. Even comes before the main verb
Your wording"I couldn't get an even job interview."
Minimal correction"I couldn't even get a job interview."
Natural version"I didn't even make it to the interview stage."
Why? For this meaning, place even before get, and use a before job interview. The natural version names the recruitment stage directly and preserves your disappointment.
2. Ask about one turnip
Your wording"What is turnips?"
Minimal correction"What are turnips?"
Natural version"What is a turnip?"
Why? The plural noun turnips takes are. Alternatively, use is with a turnip; that singular question is a natural way to ask what an unfamiliar vegetable is.
3. Ask about a total amount of candy
Your wording"Do how much candies your sons get?"
Minimal correction"How much candy did your sons get?"
Natural version"How much candy did your sons collect for Halloween?"
Why? Use how much candy for the total amount, then did + subject + get for a completed event. Remove the initial false start do. Collect for Halloween makes the situation explicit. Candies can refer to individual sweets or varieties, but the uncountable form fits this question.
4. Keep a completed story in the past
Your wording"He ate candies almost a year."
Minimal correction"He ate candies for almost a year."
Natural version"He had enough candy to last almost a year."
Why? Add for to introduce the duration in this sentence. The natural version uses enough candy to last to express your central point: the Halloween collection supplied him for almost a year.
5. Use children without a possessive ending
Your wording"Many people oversee their children's at once."
Minimal correction"Many people oversee their children at once."
Natural version"Several parents supervise their children together."
Why? Children is already plural. The possessive children’s needs a following noun, such as safety. Supervise is a more usual verb for watching children during an activity, and together captures the shared supervision you described.
6. Relate two dates with before
Your wording"A month ago of the election day."
Minimal correction"A month before election day."
Natural version"You can vote a month in advance of election day."
Why? Ago measures backward from now; it does not connect an action to a later reference event. Use before election day. The natural version turns your time phrase into a complete sentence and uses the expression Cheryl supplied, in advance.
7. Feel takes an adjective directly
Your wording"Might not feel be embarrassed."
Minimal correction"Might not feel embarrassed."
Natural version"They might not feel embarrassed."
Why? Use either feel embarrassed or be embarrassed, not feel be embarrassed. Removing be fixes the verb pattern; the natural version adds the subject from the surrounding discussion to make a complete sentence.
8. Describe a feeling with stressed
Your wording"I'm very stressful."
Minimal correction"I'm very stressed."
Natural version"I'm under a lot of stress."
Why? You used this wording while reporting your colleague’s feelings. A person who feels pressure is stressed; a situation that causes pressure is stressful. Under a lot of stress is a natural conversational phrase with the same meaning.
