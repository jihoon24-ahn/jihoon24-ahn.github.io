🔍 Highest-Priority Patterns

1. Control verb forms inside past stories

When you tell a completed story, keep the main events in the simple past. Use would for a future possibility viewed from that past time.

  • “My family was worried that my brother would never get married.”
  • “My father introduced several women to him, but he refused to meet them.”
  • “Eventually, his acquaintance introduced him to the woman he later married.”

2. Use time and place prepositions as fixed chunks

Practice the complete phrase rather than choosing each preposition separately.

  • “My wife came to Korea in August.”
  • “I stay at my parents’ house on weekends.”
  • “Street vendors were standing along the road.”

3. Learn verbs with their natural partners

Several errors came from translating one word at a time. Store the verb and its usual noun together.

  • “get married,” “do household chores,” and “make a decision”
  • “reduce the tax burden” and “contribute toward the wedding”
  • “use new appliances” and “earn a good test score”

4. Reintroduce people in a family story

Because the conversation included your brother, his wife, her cousin, and both sets of parents, pronouns sometimes became unclear. Repeat the relationship when the speaker changes.

  • “His wife’s cousin knew my brother first. She later introduced my brother to her cousin.”
  • “Before the wedding, my brother took care of our parents. Now that he lives in Daejeon, I visit them more often.”

🚀 Suggestions to Improve

Build stories with situation → complication → result

This structure will help you tell long stories without losing the listener.

Example: “My brother moved to Daejeon for work and did not know many people there. My mother worried that he would not meet anyone. However, someone from his swimming pool introduced him to her cousin, and they eventually got married.”

Use a name or relationship after every topic shift

Avoid beginning several sentences with “he,” “she,” or “they” when more than one person is involved.

Example: “My mother asked me to help my brother financially. My brother later thanked me and sent my family some wild ginseng.”

Practice difficult ideas first in plain English

Explain tax or inheritance ideas with one simple sentence before using technical vocabulary.

Example: “My parents can give us a certain amount while they are alive without creating a large tax bill. In other words, they can make gifts up to the tax-free limit.”

Describe personality with precise adjectives

Use “stubborn” or “strong-willed” for someone who rarely changes a decision. Use “calmer” or “less irritable” for an improved mood.

Example: “My brother is strong-willed, but my wife seems much calmer than she was during our time in Washington.”

✏️ Key Corrections

Tap a correction to see the revision and explanation.

1. Describe street sellers
Your wording“Actually, it was not a retail shop. In the road, many people were standing.”
Minimal correction“Actually, it was not a retail shop. Many people were standing along the road.”
Natural version“It wasn’t a regular store; it was a street vendor selling leather goods.”
Why? Use “along the road” or “on the street,” not “in the road,” unless someone is physically blocking traffic. “Street vendor” or “hawker” identifies the seller more clearly.
2. Regret a bargaining decision
Your wording“I should have offered 25, 20 euros.”
Minimal correction“I should have offered 20 or 25 euros.”
Natural version“I should have started by offering 20 euros and negotiated from there.”
Why? Join alternatives with “or.” The natural version explains the bargaining strategy and uses “negotiated from there” for the later back-and-forth.
3. Use “in” with a month
Your wording“I met my wife on August.”
Minimal correction“I met my wife in August.”
Natural version“My wife came back to Korea in August for my younger brother’s wedding.”
Why? Use “in” with months and years. Use “on” with a particular day, such as “on August 15.” The natural version also makes the event and reason explicit.
4. Explain your mother’s concern
Your wording“One of the biggest worries for her, it is my brother.”
Minimal correction“One of her biggest worries was my brother.”
Natural version“My mother had been worried that my younger brother might never get married.”
Why? Do not repeat the subject with both “one of her biggest worries” and “it.” The past tense fits because the concern existed before the wedding.
5. Use “get married” after “would”
Your wording“My family was worried that he wouldn’t married.”
Minimal correction“My family was worried that he wouldn’t get married.”
Natural version“My family was worried that he might never get married.”
Why? A modal verb such as “would” must be followed by a base verb. “Married” is not the needed base verb here, so use the fixed expression “get married.”
6. Describe travel time naturally
Your wording“It’s far from Seoul. So three hours drive.”
Minimal correction“It’s far from Seoul. It’s a three-hour drive.”
Natural version“Daejeon is about three hours away from Seoul by car.”
Why? The second part needs a subject and verb. Before a noun, “three-hour” acts as one adjective, so “hour” remains singular and the words are hyphenated.
7. Describe your weekend routine
Your wording“In weekends, I sleep at parents’ home.”
Minimal correction“On weekends, I sleep at my parents’ home.”
Natural version“I usually stay overnight at my parents’ house on weekends.”
Why? Use “on weekends” and include the possessive “my.” “Stay overnight” sounds more natural when the point is that you visit and spend the night there.
8. Talk about household chores
Your wording“My mother have me make many things, such as house chores, vacuum cleaning, and washing a dish.”
Minimal correction“My mother has me do many things, such as household chores, vacuuming, and washing the dishes.”
Natural version“When I visit, my mother asks me to help with chores such as vacuuming and washing the dishes.”
Why? Use “has” with the singular subject “mother,” “do chores” rather than “make chores,” and “wash the dishes” for the general household task.
9. Explain the tax-free limit
Your wording“My parents can choose to give me some amount of money under some limit.”
Minimal correction“My parents can choose to give me a certain amount of money under the limit.”
Natural version“My parents can give me money up to the tax-free gift limit.”
Why? “Some amount under some limit” is understandable but vague. “Up to the tax-free gift limit” expresses both the maximum amount and the tax context precisely.
10. Describe your brother’s finances
Your wording“My mother was worried about my brother’s economic background.”
Minimal correction“My mother was worried about my brother’s financial situation.”
Natural version“My mother was concerned about whether my brother could afford a home after getting married.”
Why? “Economic background” usually refers broadly to the conditions in which someone grew up. “Financial situation” means the person’s current money circumstances.
11. Explain your parents’ difficulty with technology
Your wording“They doesn’t know how to manage some electronics.”
Minimal correction“They don’t know how to use some electronic devices.”
Natural version“My parents sometimes need help using the new appliances in their apartment.”
Why? Use “don’t” with “they.” We normally “use” or “operate” devices; “manage” means control or organize something rather than operate it.
12. Explain your promotion goal
Your wording“I need the English good tests for promotion. My company needs me to get the good score.”
Minimal correction“I need a good English test score for promotion. My company needs me to get a good score.”
Natural version“I need a strong English test score to qualify for promotion, even though I rarely use English in my current role.”
Why? The natural noun phrase is “an English test score,” and a singular countable noun needs an article. “To qualify for promotion” clearly explains the score’s purpose.

💬 Useful Language

street vendor / hawker

Someone who sells goods in a public place rather than inside a regular store.

haggle over the price

Negotiate repeatedly to reach a lower price. “I should have haggled over the price.”

hit it off

Quickly form a friendly or romantic connection. “They hit it off and started dating.”

meet one’s match

Meet someone with an equally strong personality or ability.

a tax-free gift limit

The maximum gift that can be made without triggering gift tax under the applicable rules.

contribute toward something

Give money or help for a shared expense. “I contributed toward my brother’s new home.”

absence makes the heart grow fonder

Being apart can make people appreciate each other more.

catch up professionally

Reach a similar career position or level after falling behind.
