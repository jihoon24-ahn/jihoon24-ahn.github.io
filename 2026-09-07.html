✏️ Key Corrections

Tap a correction to open it.

1. Say where you are on public transportation
Your wording“I was in the bus.”
Minimal correction“I was on the bus.”
Natural version“I’m on the bus heading to work.”
Why? English normally uses “on” with buses, trains, planes, and other shared transportation. Because you were still traveling during the call, the present form is more precise.
2. Describe your Monday commute
Your wording“I thought one hour was enough to get to work, but in this morning I was wrong because it’s Monday and Monday is more traffic.”
Minimal correction“I thought one hour was enough to get to work, but this morning I was wrong because there is more traffic on Mondays.”
Natural version“I thought an hour would be enough, but the Monday-morning traffic was heavier than I expected.”
Why? Say “this morning,” not “in this morning.” A day cannot “be more traffic”; instead, there is more traffic, or the traffic is heavier.
3. Explain why you decided not to go
Your wording“Many people told me to drop it.”
Minimal correction“Many people told me not to go.”
Natural version“Several people talked me out of going.”
Why? “Drop it” usually means stop discussing or pursuing something already underway. “Talk someone out of doing something” specifically means persuade that person not to do it.
4. Describe a hypothetical experience
Your wording“If you go there in person, it must be terrible.”
Minimal correction“If you went there in person, it would be terrible.”
Natural version“The fireworks look spectacular online, but attending in person would be exhausting because of the crowds.”
Why? You were imagining an experience rather than describing a certainty, so use the hypothetical pattern “if + past, would + verb.” The natural version also explains exactly what would be difficult.
5. Name the closest stations
Your wording“During the festival, the subway near subway stations are closed.”
Minimal correction“During the festival, the nearest subway stations are closed.”
Natural version“The subway stations closest to the festival are temporarily closed.”
Why? “Near” describes location, while “nearest” identifies the closest ones. The natural version makes the relationship to the festival explicit.
6. Explain subway overcrowding
Your wording“The subway is vulnerable to crowded situation.”
Minimal correction“The subway is vulnerable in a crowded situation.”
Natural version“Underground stations can become dangerous when they are overcrowded.”
Why? A singular countable noun needs “a,” but “vulnerable in a crowded situation” still sounds vague. “Overcrowded” directly describes the unsafe condition.
7. Describe the risk to passengers
Your wording“The subway station is dangerous to oppressed people.”
Minimal correction“The subway station is dangerous because people can be crushed.”
Natural version“If too many people enter at once, passengers could be crushed in the crowd.”
Why? “Oppressed” usually refers to unfair political or social control, not physical crowd pressure. “Be crushed in the crowd” expresses the safety risk you intended.
8. Say how early people arrive
Your wording“Many people go there in advance about ten hours before.”
Minimal correction“Many people go there about ten hours in advance.”
Natural version“Some people arrive as much as ten hours early to secure a parking space.”
Why? “In advance” and “before” duplicate the same time relationship. “Arrive ten hours early” is shorter, and the natural version adds the purpose.
9. Describe the event sponsor
Your wording“The main sponsor is the Korean company who make the compounder and fireworks.”
Minimal correction“The main sponsor is a Korean company that makes explosives and fireworks.”
Natural version“The event is sponsored by a Korean company that manufactures both defense products and fireworks.”
Why? Use “that” for a company and “makes” with the singular subject. “Manufactures defense products” is clearer and more professional than the unclear transcribed word “compounder.”
10. Distinguish “doing well” from “going well”
Your wording“They are going very well in this situation under the war.”
Minimal correction“They are doing very well during the war.”
Natural version“Ironically, the company is doing very well because demand for defense products has increased.”
Why? A company “does well” when it performs successfully; an event or process “goes well.” Use “during” for the time period, not “under.”
