🚀 Suggestions to Improve

Restart immediately after an auxiliary error

If you notice a form such as “didn’t heard,” repeat the auxiliary and correct base verb immediately. A short restart is clearer than continuing with the wrong structure.

Example: “I didn’t heard—sorry, I didn’t hear about it.”

Ask who receives the action

Before choosing active or passive voice, identify whether the subject causes or receives the effect.

Example: “The smoke affects Korea, so Korea is affected by the smoke.”

Use a simple comparison frame

Build comparisons with subject, linking verb, comparative adjective, and the comparison target. This prevents missing articles and reference words.

Example: “The weather app was more accurate in the U.S. than it was in Korea.”

Prepare topic chunks before adding details

Start with two or three reliable phrases, then connect them into a longer explanation.

Example: “The government issues a fine-dust advisory, people wear masks, and schools cancel outdoor PE classes.”

Why? Use predict or forecast for weather, not expect. Information is uncountable here and does not need to be repeated.

💬 Useful Language

on the tip of my tongue

Almost remembered but temporarily unavailable. “The term was on the tip of my tongue.”

weather station

A place that measures local weather conditions. “The forecast uses data from many weather stations.”

projected trajectory

The expected path of a storm or other moving system.

be affected by

Receive an influence or consequence. “Korea can be affected by pollution from nearby regions.”

fine-dust advisory

An official notice about unhealthy levels of small airborne particles.

night owl

Someone who naturally stays awake and functions well late at night.

manage my caffeine intake

Control how much caffeine you consume and when you consume it.

designed for

Made for a particular purpose. “This watch is designed for outdoor activities.”
