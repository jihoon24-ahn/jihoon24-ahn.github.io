✏️ Key Corrections

Tap a correction to see the revision and explanation.

1. Explaining a laptop problem
Your wording"My laptop is low battery, and it is… now it… It is low power mode, so I think… It cannot turn on the camera."
Minimal correction"My laptop's battery is low, and it is in low power mode, so I think it cannot turn on the camera."
Natural version"My laptop's battery is low, so it's in low power mode and the camera won't turn on."
Why? A laptop isn't low battery; its battery is low. A device is in a mode. Won't turn on is the natural way to say a device refuses to work.
2. Describing a poll result
Your wording"In the morning, I checked the New York Times poll, and it said the blue tsunami to almost every state."
Minimal correction"In the morning, I checked the New York Times poll, and it said there would be a blue tsunami in almost every state."
Natural version"This morning, I checked the New York Times poll, and it predicted a blue wave in almost every state."
Why? Said needs a full clause after it, and states take in, not to. Predicted is the precise verb for polls.
3. Saying the polls were wrong before
Your wording"But, you know, two years ago — No. I fooled."
Minimal correction"But two years ago, I was fooled."
Natural version"But two years ago, the polls fooled me."
Why? I fooled means you tricked someone. You need the passive I was fooled, or make the polls the subject: the polls fooled me.
4. Explaining Korean interest in the election
Your wording"Even in Korea, many people are curious about the midterm later, because of the stock market."
Minimal correction"Even in Korea, many people are curious about the midterms because of the stock market."
Natural version"Even in Korea, people are watching the midterms closely because of the stock market."
Why? The U.S. elections are usually the midterms, plural. Later adds nothing here. Watch closely is a strong collocation for following news.
5. Introducing a video you watched
Your wording"About an hour, I see your video on Zoom event."
Minimal correction"About an hour ago, I saw a video."
Natural version"About an hour ago, I watched a video about this."
Why? Use ago for a time in the past, and the past tense saw or watched. Your video means Catherine's video; you meant a video.
6. Describing voting in Ohio
Your wording"In Ohio State, there is no, ah, there is only one… Only one. Only pouring… Voting, only voting. Area for early voting center."
Minimal correction"In Ohio, there is only one early voting center per county."
Natural version"In Ohio, there's only one early voting center per county."
Why? Ohio State usually means the university; the state is just Ohio. Say one … per county once, in a single complete sentence, instead of repeating only one.
7. Reporting a claim
Your wording"They argued, they said, they… is insist. Republicans ruin this country so they they don't want to lose so they make made only one."
Minimal correction"They insisted that Republicans don't want to lose, so they made only one."
Natural version"They claimed that Republicans set up only one voting station per county because they don't want to lose."
Why? Insist is a verb, so use they insisted, not is insist. Pick one reporting verb and one past verb (made, not make made). Claimed that fits a claim you have not checked yet.
8. Saying what you want to talk about
Your wording"Because I'm really looking forward to the result of election."
Minimal correction"Because I'm really looking forward to the result of the election."
Natural version"I'm really looking forward to the election results."
Why? Election needs the here. The election results is the natural compound noun, shorter than the result of the election.
