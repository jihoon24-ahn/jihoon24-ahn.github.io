🔍 Highest-Priority Patterns

1. Use articles with singular countable nouns

Singular nouns such as day, ceremony, soulmate, and excuse usually need a, an, or the.

  • “The 49th day is an important day in the tradition.”
  • “I believe that a soulmate can also be a close friend.”

2. Separate states from completed actions

Use have been for a continuing state and a simple or present-perfect verb for actions and experiences.

  • “I have been married for about five years.”
  • “Over that time, I have observed many couples.”

4. Organize abstract opinions with clear frames

🚀 Suggestions to Improve

Define a tradition in three steps

Give the name, timing, and meaning before adding family details. This creates a clear path for the listener.

Example: “It is a Buddhist memorial ceremony. It takes place around the 49th day after someone passes away. The day has spiritual significance for the family.”

Anchor continuing states with “have been”

When a situation began in the past and continues now, start with have been and add for plus a duration.

Example: “I have been married for about five years, and my views on relationships have changed.”

Use a whether-or frame for uncertainty

A whether…or… frame lets you compare possibilities without creating several unfinished clauses.

Example: “An outsider cannot know whether it is a genuine connection or simply an excuse.”

Compare cultures with one contrast at a time

State the practice in one culture, then contrast the likely reaction in the other culture.

Example: “Roasts are a familiar part of some American award shows, whereas public jokes about a guest may feel rude in Korea.”

1. Name the memorial ceremony clearly
Your wording“Have you heard about 49 ceremony, funeral ceremony?”
Minimal correction“Have you heard about the 49-day funeral ceremony?”
Natural version“Have you heard of the Buddhist 49-day memorial ceremony?”
Why? A specific ceremony needs the, and 49-day works as a compound adjective. Memorial ceremony is more precise and less likely to suggest that the funeral itself lasts 49 days.
2. Use an article before “important day”
Your wording“Forty-nine is seven times seven, so it’s a little bit important day.”
Minimal correction“Forty-nine is seven times seven, so it’s a little bit of an important day.”
Natural version“Forty-nine is seven times seven, so the 49th day has special significance.”
Why? A singular countable noun needs an article. The natural version replaces the awkward degree phrase with has special significance, which suits a cultural explanation.
3. Avoid the progressive with a future decision
Your wording“On the 49th day, she will be deciding where she goes.”
Minimal correction“On the 49th day, she will decide where she goes.”
Natural version“On the 49th day, it is believed that her soul’s next destination is decided.”
Why? Use will decide for a single future event. The natural version uses an impersonal belief frame and passive voice to explain the tradition without presenting it as an objective fact.
4. Use “take” with “seriously”
Your wording“Many people don’t think this 49th ceremony very seriously.”
Minimal correction“Many people don’t take this 49-day ceremony very seriously.”
Natural version“Many people no longer observe the 49-day ceremony strictly.”
Why? English uses the collocation take something seriously, not think something seriously. Observe a ceremony means to follow or perform the tradition.
5. Explain a non-romantic soulmate
Your wording“I don’t think it’s the close meaning in romantic relationships. I think we can use this word in friends.”
Minimal correction“I don’t think its meaning is limited to romantic relationships. I think we can use this word for friends.”
Natural version“I don’t think soulmates have to be romantic partners; close friends can be soulmates too.”
Why? Use be limited to to restrict a meaning and for friends rather than in friends. The natural version states the opinion directly and supports it with a clear contrast.
6. Use the present simple for a habit
Your wording“I sometimes saw them.”
Minimal correction“I sometimes watch them.”
Natural version“I watch romantic comedies sometimes.”
Why? Use the present simple for a current habit. Saw describes a completed past event, while watch fits repeated viewing.
7. Use “whether” to compare possibilities
Your wording“The person doesn’t know it is just excuse or it is lie or they are really soulmate.”
Minimal correction“The person doesn’t know whether it is just an excuse or a lie, or whether they are really soulmates.”
Natural version“No one else can know whether it is an excuse or a genuine connection.”
Why? Use whether when presenting alternatives, and add articles before singular nouns. The natural version removes repetition and contrasts the two central possibilities.
8. Use a real condition for meeting a soulmate
Your wording“If soulmate exists, I would know she is my soulmate immediately.”
Minimal correction“If a soulmate exists, I would know she is my soulmate immediately.”
Natural version“If I met my soulmate, I think I would know immediately.”
Why? Add a before the singular noun. The natural version uses if + past with would to describe an imagined future situation.
9. Describe a continuing marital state
Your wording“I have married for over five years.”
Minimal correction“I have been married for over five years.”
Natural version“I’ve been married for about five years.”
Why? Marry is an event, while be married is a state. Use the present perfect form have been married with for to show that the state continues now.
10. Remove the passive form from “watch”
Your wording“I’ve been watched many couples.”
Minimal correction“I’ve watched many couples.”
Natural version“Over the years, I’ve observed many couples and noticed that every relationship has problems.”
Why? You performed the action, so use the active present perfect have watched, not have been watched. Observed better expresses learning from other couples over time.

💬 Useful Language

49-day memorial ceremony

A Buddhist observance held around the 49th day after a person’s death.

pass away

A respectful way to say that someone died. “My grandmother passed away 49 days ago.”

be reincarnated

Be born again as another person or living being according to a religious belief.

get together

Meet as a group. “Saturday was the only day when the whole family could get together.”

the real deal

Something or someone genuine rather than an imitation or excuse.

bound to happen

Certain or very likely to happen. “Disagreements are bound to happen because no one is perfect.”

a roast

An event or performance in which people make planned jokes about a particular person.

be a good sport

React pleasantly when teased or when something does not go your way.

What I believe about soulmates

I believe in soulmates, but I don’t think a soulmate has to be a romantic partner. A close friend can also understand you deeply and become an important part of your life. At the same time, I no longer believe that meeting a soulmate guarantees a perfect relationship. I’ve been married for about five years, and I’ve observed many couples. Every relationship has its own problems because no one is perfect. In fact, being with someone who is exactly like you might be boring because you could predict everything they would do. Differences can make a relationship more interesting, although they can also cause conflict. If someone who is already married says they have met a soulmate, an outsider cannot know whether the connection is genuine or simply an excuse. Ultimately, I think a strong relationship depends less on finding one perfect person and more on understanding differences, solving problems, and continuing to care for each other.
