🔍 Highest-Priority Patterns

1. Use “in” for a future time

Use “in + amount of time” when you mean how long from now. Use “later” when the starting point is already clear.

  • “One of my coworkers is getting married in two months.”
  • “We can hold the bachelor party later, after his son feels better.”

2. Control agreement and articles

Check the subject before choosing “is” or “are”, and use “a” or “an” with one countable item.

  • “One of my coworkers is getting married.”
  • “We usually give each guest a wedding invitation.”

3. Use natural event collocations

Learn a noun together with the verb that commonly goes with it.

  • “We were going to have a small gathering.”
  • “The bride and groom usually treat their friends to dinner.”

4. Choose the tense before adding details

Use the present perfect for an unfinished decision and the simple past for a completed situation.

  • “We haven't decided when we're going to meet yet.”
  • “I got married during the COVID-19 pandemic.”

🚀 Suggestions to Improve

Use a three-part cultural explanation

State the custom, explain the reason, and give one example.

Example: “In Korea, couples often meet friends before the wedding. They usually pay for dinner because the guests will later bring a cash gift. For example, my wife and I handed out many invitations in person.”

Build a time-expression bank

Practice each phrase with one real sentence.

Examples: “in two months,” “during the pandemic,” “before my wedding,” and “we haven't decided yet.”

Replace translated collocations

Record the complete replacement—not only one word.

Example: Change “make a gathering” to “have a gathering”, and change “return my investment” to “receive some of that money back.”

Finish the core sentence first

When an answer becomes complicated, finish the main fact and begin a second sentence.

Example: “Our gathering was postponed. One coworker's son had a high fever, so we decided to meet another time.”

Korean wedding invitations

“In Korea, couples often meet their friends before the wedding and hand out invitations in person. The bride and groom usually treat everyone to dinner or lunch.
